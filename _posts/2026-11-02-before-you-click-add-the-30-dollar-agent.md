Thirty dollars a month is a small number next to a missed call. It is also enough to put a stranger in your Yelp thread, talking to a homeowner about price and timing, before you have read the request.

That is the decision. The subscription is the easy part.

## What just got offered to a local pro

On October 20, 2026, Yelp announced [Smart Lead Reply](https://www.yelp-ir.com/news/press-releases/news-release-details/2026/Yelp-Announces-More-Personalized-Yelp-Assistant-Agent-that-Checks-Restaurant-Wait-Times-and-AI-Tools-for-Service-Professionals/default.aspx) in its fall product release. The press release says it rolls out the following month at $30 per month for businesses in eligible categories that use Request a Quote, including movers, roofers, plumbers, home cleaners, contractors, electricians, and additional services businesses. Yelp's [product post](https://blog.yelp.com/news/fall-product-release-2026/) says the same price, inside the Yelp Business Owner Account.

The behavior is specific. A customer submits a project through Request a Quote. Smart Lead Reply answers in the message thread, asks about the project, and qualifies the lead. You can load a scheduling link, the services you offer, pricing, promos, questions to ask, and preferences for how the conversation should go. Once the lead is qualified, it can share the scheduling link so the customer books on your existing calendar tool. Yelp says every conversation is logged in your Yelp inbox, with a notification to review the lead and pick up where the agent left off.

"The following month," counted from October 20, is November 2026. I have not confirmed that the switch is live for every eligible category on the day this was written.

Two days later, on October 22, Roofr announced an [AI Receptionist](https://roofr.com/blog/roofr-announces-new-ai-receptionist-and-more-at-roofr-builds-fall-2026) for its roofing CRM. The announcement says it qualifies leads, books appointments, and routes urgent calls. The announcement does not list a price. On Roofr's [product page](https://roofr.com/ai-receptionist), checked for this essay, the listed price is $99 a month including 200 minutes, cancel anytime, with Essentials and Scale customers free until billing starts January 1, 2027. The page also says overages are not charged right now, and that Roofr will say so in advance if that changes. Product pages move. Read yours on the day you decide.

The [Main Street AI Brief for October 26](https://agenticsolutions.biz/brief/2026-10-26) put the two offers side by side for local owners. The Yelp price and the November timing match Yelp's own release.

## Most owners are picturing a chatbot

Network Solutions published a [survey of 187 American small-business owners who already use AI](https://www.networksolutions.com/blog/small-business-ai-usage-survey/), run online by Fractl in September 2026. A small business, in their definition, has 50 or fewer employees. The typical respondent was a solo owner. The margin of error is about 7.2 points. Fractl ran it for Network Solutions, among owners who already use an AI tool.

When those owners were asked to describe an AI agent in their own words, about 32% described software that takes action or completes tasks on its own. About 66% described a chatbot, an assistant, or a search tool. Another 3% were not sure. Even among the 31% who said they had used an agent, fewer than two in five described one that way.

That gap is the buying problem. "Add the AI" sounds like a helper that drafts a reply you will send. Smart Lead Reply, as Yelp describes it, steps into the thread right away. Roofr's product page says the receptionist books a home visit with every new caller. The customer hears a commitment. You hear about it afterward, in a notification or a CRM record.

Quoting, qualifying, and booking are three jobs that happen to share a phone number. [Your First AI Agent Should Do One Job](/posts/your-first-ai-should-do-one-job/) is the narrower start: one trigger, one output, one way to take it back.

## Five questions before the switch goes on

Write the answers down. If a question has no answer, leave the feature off.

**1. What can it quote?**

Yelp lets you load pricing and promos. If you load a number, assume the agent can say that number. A published trip fee for a drain cleaning can be sayable. A roof leak, a panel upgrade, or a move with stairs usually needs a site visit, and the only safe line is that you have to see the job. Roofr's product page ties a ballpark phone quote to Instant Estimator and to your pricing. Stale pricing becomes a quote in your voice.

Write the prices it may say, the prices it must leave alone, and the sentence it uses when the answer is "it depends."

**2. Who sees the lead first?**

On Yelp's description, the agent talks to the customer in the thread, then you get a notification to review the qualified lead. The customer may already have a scheduling link. That order is reasonable for a gutter cleaning you are happy to book. It is a bad order for a job you would have declined, or for a customer you already know is difficult, or for an address you do not serve.

Decide, per job type, whether the agent may speak before a person has seen the request. Name the person, and name how fast they look. "Someone will check the inbox" leaves both blank.

**3. What happens off-hours?**

A 9pm "we can be there Thursday" is a promise. If the crew is full, the agent just sold a slot you do not have. Set the hours it answers, the days it is allowed to offer, and the sentence it uses when the calendar is full. "We'll call you in the morning" is a fine sentence if a person actually calls.

For a phone agent, call it yourself after hours. Use a leak, a no-heat, or a price question you would not want answered from the website alone. See whether it books, takes a message, or invents a day.

**4. How do you correct it?**

One wrong price in a Yelp thread is now in the customer's messages. Deleting your copy does not delete theirs. Write the correction you will send, who sends it, and how fast. The same page covers a booked inspection that should have stayed a message. Name the screen and the person. [Where Human Review Actually Belongs](/posts/where-human-review-belongs/) is where that person sits: at the step where a draft becomes something the customer can hold you to.

Write the sentence before you need it. "We will apologize if it happens" leaves the callback unstaffed.

**5. How do you turn it off?**

Find the off switch before you turn it on. Canceling a subscription is one switch. Pausing it for a storm week, or for the day your dispatcher is out, is a different switch. Yelp's October posts describe customization, a logged thread, and a review notification. In the sections I read, they do not describe the off switch. Ask support where it is, and click it once while the feature is still quiet.

Roofr's page says cancel anytime, and Essentials and Scale start billing on January 1, 2027 unless you cancel first. Put that date on a calendar. A free period that becomes $99 on a date is a switch with a deadline.

## The $30 is the small line

In [Agent ROI Is Missing a Line Item](/posts/agent-roi-is-missing-a-line-item/) the subscription is the number everyone writes down. The hours you spend checking, fixing, and calling people back are the number that decides whether the tool was cheap.

Thirty dollars is one trip fee. A booked day you cannot staff, or a price a customer screenshots, costs more than the subscription. If the five answers fit on a page and a person has called the agent as a customer, turn it on for one job type. If they do not, wait. The button will still be there.
